AI agents for accounts receivable collections work the overdue list every day: they send the right follow-up to the right contact, attach the proof of delivery or pricing a customer needs, log promises to pay, and route real disputes to your team with the evidence ready. For distributors and manufacturers, that turns collections from a weekly scramble into a steady, documented process.

## Why do invoices go past due at distributors?

Ask a credit manager at a wholesale or industrial distributor why an invoice is 45 days late and the answer is rarely "the customer refuses to pay." Far more often it is one of these:

- The customer's AP team is waiting on a signed proof of delivery that sits in your carrier's portal.
- A line was invoiced at list price instead of the contract price, so the buyer parked the whole invoice.
- The invoice was emailed to a person who left the company, or was never uploaded to the customer's supplier portal.
- A partial shipment created two invoices and the customer can only match one to their purchase order.

Each of these is a small fix. The problem is volume. A $30M distributor might carry several thousand open invoices across hundreds of accounts, and a two-person AR team cannot chase every one of them with the care each needs. So they prioritize the biggest balances, send generic reminders on the rest, and the small stalled invoices age quietly into the 60 and 90 day buckets.

The pattern is widespread. [PYMNTS reported in 2025](https://www.pymnts.com/accounts-receivable/2025/for-86percent-of-firms-nearly-a-third-of-invoiced-sales-are-late/) that 86% of businesses say up to 30% of their monthly invoiced sales are overdue, and that more than 75% of small and mid-sized businesses still chase collections or handle disputes by email. The same piece found 27% of respondents spend at least half of their AR team's time resolving invoice disputes.

## What does a manual collections desk look like day to day?

Most mid-size distributors run collections from an aging report, an inbox and memory. On Monday someone exports the aging, sorts by balance, and starts calling. Notes go into the ERP comment field when there is time, or into a spreadsheet, or nowhere. When a customer says "send me the POD," the specialist has to leave the call, log into the carrier or WMS, find the document, and email it back. That single request can take twenty minutes, and it may happen a dozen times a day.

The result is uneven treatment. Large accounts get attention. Small accounts with many little invoices, which often carry the most disputes per dollar, get a templated reminder every few weeks. Credit holds land on customers who already paid, and customers who should be on hold keep ordering because nobody noticed their promise to pay passed two weeks ago.

## How do AI agents change accounts receivable collections?

An AI agent is software that can read, decide on a next step within rules you set, and act across your systems. On a collections desk, that means it does the legwork a specialist would do before every follow-up, for every open invoice, every day.

| Collections task | Manual AR desk | AR desk with AI agents |
|---|---|---|
| Deciding who to chase | Sorted by balance once a week | Every invoice scored daily by age, history and dispute risk |
| First reminder | Generic template, sent in batches | Account-specific message to the current AP contact, timed to their payment cycle |
| "Send me the POD" | Specialist searches carrier portal and WMS | Agent pulls the signed POD and replies within minutes |
| Pricing disputes | Found when the customer calls | Flagged before the invoice ages, with the contract price attached for review |
| Supplier portal uploads | Done when someone remembers | Tracked per customer, with missing uploads surfaced |
| Promises to pay | Noted in a comment field, if at all | Logged with a date, followed up automatically if missed |
| Credit hold decisions | Based on last week's aging | Based on current balance, promises and open disputes |

The important column is not the speed. It is consistency. Every invoice gets the same diligence that your best specialist gives the top ten accounts, and your team spends its hours on the conversations that need a person: a genuine pricing disagreement, a customer in financial trouble, a relationship worth protecting.

We still keep the judgment with your people. Write-offs, credit limit changes, settlement offers and anything that touches a strategic account stay with your credit manager. The agent prepares the case and the draft; a person approves it.

## Is AI collections different from dunning software?

Yes, and the difference matters for distributors. Traditional dunning tools send a fixed sequence of reminders: 7 days past due, 15 days, 30 days. They work well when the reason for non-payment is forgetfulness. They do very little when the real reason is a missing document or a pricing error, because the reminder does not fix anything. The customer's AP clerk receives a fourth automated email about an invoice they cannot approve, and ignores it.

An AI agent reads the customer's reply, works out what is actually blocking payment, and goes and gets it. If the reply says the PO number is wrong, it checks the order and drafts a corrected invoice for approval. If the reply says the goods arrived damaged, it opens the claim path and alerts the right person on your team. That is the step dunning tools were never designed to take, and it is where most of the aging actually sits.

If you have read our piece on [what AI automation does for distributor cash application](/blog/ai-automation-distributor-cash-application), this is the other half of the same cycle. Cash application makes sure payments that arrive get posted. Collections makes sure the payments that have not arrived get unblocked.

## What are the new late payment rules in the UK, and why do they matter?

For distributors selling into the UK, the pressure on collections is also regulatory. The government's [Commercial Payments Bill](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/commercial-payments-bill-factsheets/commercial-payments-bill-overview), introduced to Parliament in May 2026, proposes maximum payment terms of 60 days, mandatory interest on late payments at 8% above the Bank of England base rate, and a fixed sum owed to suppliers when a purchaser raises a dispute late or without enough information. The government estimates late payment costs the UK economy £11 billion a year and sees 38 UK businesses close every day.

Two things follow for a supplier. First, the rules reward suppliers who can show exactly when an invoice was sent, what was disputed, and when. That is an audit trail most manual desks do not keep. Second, if your customers are pushed toward faster payment, the suppliers whose invoices are clean and complete get paid first. Clean paperwork becomes a competitive advantage.

US, Canadian and Australian distributors are not under the same bill, but the same logic applies to any large customer with strict AP controls. Their systems pay what they can match and park what they cannot.

## Which distributors see the biggest gains from AI agents in AR?

The return is highest where the invoice count is high and the dollar value per invoice is modest. In our experience that profile includes:

1. **Industrial and MRO distributors** with thousands of small invoices to contractors and plants, many of them tied to partial shipments.
2. **Food, beverage and foodservice distributors** where deductions for shortages, damages and promotions are routine.
3. **Building products and electrical distributors** selling to contractors whose AP teams require job numbers, lien paperwork or portal uploads before they release payment.
4. **Manufacturers selling through retail or big-box channels** where every invoice must match a retailer's PO and ASN exactly.

What these share is not size. It is the number of small, fixable reasons an invoice can stall. Those are the reasons an AI agent can work through tirelessly, and the reasons a lean AR team has to triage.

## What should an operations leader measure before changing anything?

You can size the opportunity from data you already have. Three numbers tell most of the story:

1. **The share of your 31 to 90 day balance that is tied to a dispute or missing document**, rather than a customer with cash trouble. If your team cannot answer this quickly, that alone is a finding.
2. **Average days from a customer raising a dispute to it being resolved.** Long resolution times usually point to document retrieval, not negotiation.
3. **How many accounts received no personal contact last month** despite having overdue invoices. This shows how much of the ledger is being triaged rather than worked.

Distributors that pull these numbers usually discover the same thing: the money is not lost, it is stuck, and the reasons are repetitive enough to hand to software.
